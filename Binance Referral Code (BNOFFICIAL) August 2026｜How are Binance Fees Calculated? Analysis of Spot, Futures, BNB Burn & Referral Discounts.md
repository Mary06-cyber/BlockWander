## 📈 2. How are Binance Spot Trading Fees Calculated?

Spot fees hinge on two roles: **Maker** and **Taker**.

### What is a Maker?
A Maker **places a limit order that waits to be filled.**
Example: Setting a buy order for BTC at a specific low price. This order sits in the order book, providing liquidity.
*Generally, Maker fees are lower.*

### What is a Taker?
A Taker **executes an order immediately against existing orders.**
Example: Using a Market Order to buy BTC instantly. This removes liquidity from the book.
*Generally, Taker fees are higher.*

> Professional traders often use "limit orders" (Maker) to reduce transaction costs.

## 💰 3. How are Binance Futures Trading Fees Calculated?

Futures differ from Spot. Key components include:
-   Maker Fee
-   Taker Fee
-   Funding Rate

### Maker / Taker
Same logic as Spot:
-   **Maker:** Adds liquidity (Limit orders).
-   **Taker:** Removes liquidity (Market orders).

### Funding Rate
Perpetual contracts have no expiry. The Funding Rate mechanism keeps contract prices aligned with Spot prices.
-   **Positive Rate:** Longs pay Shorts.
-   **Negative Rate:** Shorts pay Longs.

> Note: Funding rates are not platform fees but affect your P&L during holding periods.
